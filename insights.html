Rugby Union

Scrums, Rucks and Mauls: A Beginner's Guide

October 12, 2026 · Sample editorial

Three set-piece contests explained in plain language, with what to watch for in each.

Read More

A scrum restarts play after a minor infringement such as a knock-on. Eight forwards from each team bind together and push for the ball, which the halfback feeds into the tunnel between them. A ruck forms when the ball is on the ground and players from both teams contest it on their feet, while a maul forms when a player carrying the ball is held up by opponents and teammates bind on. The common thread is that players must stay on their feet and join from behind the ball. Knowing which of the three is happening makes the referee's decisions far easier to follow.

Rugby Union

How Scoring Works in Rugby Union

October 10, 2026 · Sample editorial

Tries, conversions, penalties and drop goals, and how they add up on the scoreboard.

Read More

A try is worth five points and is scored by grounding the ball over the opposing try line. After a try, the scoring team can attempt a conversion kick for two extra points. A penalty goal or a drop goal is worth three points. Matches are played over two halves of 40 minutes, and teams can earn bonus points in some competitions for scoring several tries or losing narrowly. These details differ between competitions, so always check the rules of the one you follow.

Cricket

Test, One-Day and T20: Understanding Cricket's Formats

October 8, 2026 · Sample editorial

How the three main formats of cricket differ in length, tactics and feel.

Read More

Test cricket is played over up to five days, with each team batting twice, and a draw is possible. One-day cricket limits each side to a fixed number of overs in a single innings, and T20 shortens that further to twenty overs per side. Shorter formats reward big hitting and quick running, while Tests reward patience and tactics over several sessions. Many players and fans have a favourite format, and the best way to find yours is to watch a full match of each.

Cricket

A Plain-Language Cricket Glossary

October 6, 2026 · Sample editorial

Overs, wickets, maidens and more, defined without the jargon.

Read More

An over is six legal deliveries bowled from one end. A wicket falls when a batter is dismissed, and a team's innings ends when ten wickets have fallen or the overs run out. A maiden is an over in which the batting side scores no runs. A boundary scores four runs if the ball reaches the rope along the ground and six if it clears it on the full. Understanding these few terms lets a newcomer follow most commentary.

Netball

Netball Positions and Where They Can Go

October 4, 2026 · Sample editorial

Seven positions, each restricted to certain areas of the court, explained one by one.

Read More

Each netball team has seven players, and each position is restricted to certain thirds of the court. Only the goal shooter and goal attack can score, and only from inside the shooting circle. The wing attack and centre feed the ball into the attacking third, while the wing defence, goal defence and goal keeper try to intercept and defend. Players may not run with the ball and must pass it within a few seconds, which makes accurate, quick passing the heart of the game.

Netball

Footwork, Contact and Obstruction in Netball

October 2, 2026 · Sample editorial

The rules that give netball its distinctive flow, and why umpires blow the whistle so often.

Read More

Netball players may not move after taking possession of the ball, so footwork rules control where a player may land and pivot. Contact is restricted: a defender must stay a set distance from the player with the ball, and players may not bump or hold. These limits mean skill and timing matter more than strength. Understanding them explains most of the whistles heard in a match.

Football

Football Basics: Offside, Set Pieces and Added Time

September 30, 2026 · Sample editorial

The three ideas new fans ask about most, explained clearly.

Read More

A player is offside if they are nearer to the opposition goal line than both the ball and the second-last defender when the ball is played to them, with some exceptions. Set pieces such as corners and free kicks create many chances, so teams practise them often. Added time, sometimes called stoppage time, makes up for delays in each half and is decided by the referee. These three ideas answer most of the questions people ask when they start watching.

Basketball

Basketball 101: Positions, Fouls and the Shot Clock

September 28, 2026 · Sample editorial

A short guide to how basketball is organised, scored and refereed.

Read More

Each team has five players on court, and the aim is to shoot the ball through the opposing hoop. A shot is worth two points from inside the arc and three from beyond it, and free throws are worth one point. A shot clock limits how long a team can hold the ball before shooting, which keeps play fast. Fouls give the other team free throws once a team reaches its foul limit, so discipline matters.

Sailing

Sailing Terms Every Newcomer Should Know

September 26, 2026 · Sample editorial

Port, starboard, tacking and more, explained for anyone watching a regatta.

Read More

Port is the left side of a boat when you face forward and starboard is the right. To tack is to turn the bow through the wind, and to gybe is to turn the stern through it. Windward is toward the wind and leeward is away from it. Races follow a course of marks that boats round in order, and priority rules decide who must give way when boats meet. Knowing these terms makes it much easier to follow a regatta.

Explainers

Reading a Sports Schedule: NZST, NZDT and Time Zones

September 24, 2026 · Sample editorial

New Zealand's clocks change twice a year. Here is how to avoid missing a start time.

Read More

New Zealand Standard Time is 12 hours ahead of UTC, and New Zealand Daylight Time is 13 hours ahead, used from late September to early April. The Chatham Islands keep their own time, 45 minutes ahead of the main islands. Fixtures involving overseas teams often list more than one time zone, so check the label carefully. Start times can also change, so always confirm with the organiser before making plans.
