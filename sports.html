Introduction

A practical guide to the sports Kiwis follow

Each section below describes the basics of the game, the New Zealand context, major competitions and a few terms that help when you watch or read about the sport. League structures change, so always confirm details with the official organisation.

Rugby Union

Rugby Union in New Zealand

Rugby union is woven into New Zealand life. From club grounds on Saturday mornings to the country's largest stadiums, the game brings communities together. Two teams of fifteen try to ground the ball over the opposition try line, and play combines power, handling and tactical kicking.

New Zealand Rugby is the national governing body, and provincial unions run the community and provincial game. Pathways run from school and club rugby through provincial competitions to Super Rugby Pacific and international rugby. Women's rugby has grown strongly, with the Black Ferns a prominent national side.

Basic rules and formats

  • Fifteen players per side
  • Two halves of 40 minutes
  • Try = 5 points, conversion = 2, penalty or drop goal = 3
  • The ball may only be passed backwards or sideways

Major competitions

  • Super Rugby Pacific
  • Bunnings NPC and other provincial competitions
  • Rugby World Cup
  • The Rugby Championship
  • Bledisloe Cup

How to follow

Official competition websites publish fixtures, tables and team lists. Free-to-air and subscription broadcasters carry many matches, and local clubs often welcome new spectators and volunteers.

Useful terminology

Try
Grounding the ball over the opponents' line for five points
Ruck
A contest for the ball on the ground
Scrum
A set piece restart involving the forwards
Lineout
A restart after the ball goes into touch

Cricket

Cricket in New Zealand

Cricket is the summer game of New Zealand, played on village greens, school grounds and international venues. One team bats and tries to score runs while the other bowls and fields, and matches range from a few hours to several days.

New Zealand Cricket governs the sport nationally, supported by regional associations and thousands of clubs. The national men's and women's teams, the Black Caps and the White Ferns, play in all three international formats.

Basic rules and formats

  • Eleven players per side
  • An over is six legal deliveries
  • Formats include Test, one-day and T20
  • Ten wickets end an innings

Major competitions

  • International Test, one-day and T20 matches
  • Domestic first-class and limited-overs competitions
  • T20 franchise and club competitions
  • ICC World Cups

How to follow

NZ Cricket and ICC websites publish schedules and scorecards. Start times depend on the format and the weather, so check the day before travelling to a ground.

Useful terminology

Over
Six legal deliveries from one end
Wicket
A batter's dismissal, or the stumps themselves
Maiden
An over with no runs scored
Boundary
A shot reaching the rope for four or six

Netball

Netball in New Zealand

Netball is one of New Zealand's most played team sports. Two teams of seven try to pass the ball down the court and shoot goals from within the shooting circle, with players restricted to certain areas of the court.

Netball New Zealand governs the sport, and regional centres run local competitions. The national team, the Silver Ferns, is a leading side, and the domestic ANZ Premiership showcases elite players.

Basic rules and formats

  • Seven players per side
  • Four quarters of 15 minutes
  • No running with the ball
  • Goals can only be scored from inside the shooting circle

Major competitions

  • ANZ Premiership
  • Netball World Cup
  • Constellation Cup
  • National and regional club competitions

How to follow

Netball New Zealand and league websites publish fixtures, and many matches are broadcast. Local centres list junior and club competitions for those who want to play.

Useful terminology

Centre pass
The pass that starts play at the start of a quarter and after each goal
Shooting circle
The area from which goals may be scored
Intercept
Catching a pass meant for an opponent
Footwork
Rules about moving after receiving the ball

Football

Football in New Zealand

Football, known as soccer in many English-speaking countries, is played by communities across New Zealand. Two teams of eleven try to score with their feet over two halves, and the game is popular with players of all ages.

New Zealand Football is the national governing body, with regional federations running local leagues. New Zealand sides play in domestic competitions and in the Australian-based A-League, and national teams compete internationally.

Basic rules and formats

  • Eleven players per side
  • Two halves of 45 minutes plus added time
  • Offside restricts attackers
  • Cards signal cautions and sendings off

Major competitions

  • A-League Men and Women
  • New Zealand National League
  • FIFA World Cup and Women's World Cup
  • OFC competitions

How to follow

Club and league websites list fixtures and tables. Many clubs run junior programmes, and supporter groups are a friendly way to learn the game.

Useful terminology

Clean sheet
A match where a team does not concede
Set piece
A corner, free kick or throw-in
Added time
Time added at the end of a half
Offside
A positional rule for attackers

Basketball

Basketball in New Zealand

Basketball is fast, skilled and easy to start playing. Two teams try to shoot a ball through a hoop, and the sport suits school gyms, community courts and large arenas alike.

Basketball New Zealand governs the sport nationally. The national men's and women's teams, the Tall Blacks and Tall Ferns, compete internationally, and New Zealand also has a presence in the Australian-based NBL.

Basic rules and formats

  • Five players per side on court
  • FIBA games use four 10-minute quarters
  • A shot clock limits possession
  • Three-point line rewards longer shots

Major competitions

  • NBL (with New Zealand participation)
  • National Basketball League
  • FIBA World Cup and Olympic tournaments
  • Secondary school championships

How to follow

League websites publish schedules, and many regions list school and club competitions. Watching a few full games makes patterns such as the pick and roll easier to see.

Useful terminology

Pick and roll
A screen followed by a roll to the basket
Triple-double
Ten or more in three categories in one game
Fast break
A quick attack before the defence sets
Travelling
Taking too many steps without dribbling

Sailing

Sailing in New Zealand

Sailing is part of New Zealand's identity as a seafaring nation. Races take place on harbours, lakes and open water, from small dinghies to large yachts, and the sport rewards skill, teamwork and a feel for the wind.

Yachting New Zealand governs the sport, and clubs around the coast run regular racing and youth programmes. New Zealand teams have had a major profile in international events such as the America's Cup.

Basic rules and formats

  • Boats follow a course of marks
  • Priority rules decide who gives way
  • Results are based on finishing order or handicap
  • Weather can change or cancel races

Major competitions

  • America's Cup
  • SailGP
  • Olympic sailing classes
  • National and regional championships

How to follow

Event websites publish race schedules and live tracking maps. Many yacht clubs welcome visitors and run learn-to-sail courses.

Useful terminology

Port and starboard
Left and right sides of the boat
Tack
To turn the bow through the wind
Windward
The side facing the wind
Leeward
The side away from the wind
